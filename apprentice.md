# Apprentice — Ashley

## HARD RULE
Ashley's **sex/gender is never revealed** in any material — narrative, dialogue, description. All pronouns are **they/them**. Any descriptive detail (clothing, voice, body, mannerism) must remain ambiguous so any reader can map themselves onto Ashley. **This is a load-bearing constraint** for every file and any future writing.

## Core
- **Name:** Ashley
- **Age:** Mid-20s
- **Profile:** Sharp and earnest. Functions as the player's likely viewpoint into Wren's world.
- **Education:** **Bachelor's degree in psychology**, recently completed. Theoretically grounded in the field, with a stated desire to be a clinician.
- **Origin:** Came to Wren as a patient, suffering **unresolved grief over their mother**.
- **Time at the house:** roughly a year as apprentice, dating from the end of their own treatment. Established, but **still feels like a newcomer.**

## The mother
Ashley's mother **disappeared.** No body, no certainty, no funeral that meant anything. The grief is for someone Ashley couldn't bury — closure was never available in the real world. This is exactly the kind of case the device is uniquely suited to address: it had to *fabricate* what reality had denied.

## Their treatment
- Ashley came to Wren believing they were getting **hypnotherapy**. They consented to that, not to what actually happened.
- They were hypnotised, sedated, and connected to the device without their knowledge — the standard patient deception.
- **Wren entered the dream-world alone**, with no one monitoring from outside. He broke the safety rule for them.
- Wren told Ashley the treatment was **clean** — the work resolved their grief without unintended damage.
- Under the device's actual logic, every session writes onto the patient. *Clean* means within tolerance — no detectable harm, no displacement, the change limited to what was intended. It does not mean nothing was done. Ashley was changed by the session, and the change is inseparable from the healing.
- Ashley learned the truth about the device **after the fact** — after they were already healed, already grateful, already changed. They had to come to terms with the fact that they had been deceived, and that the same device had a precedent for harm Wren had not told them about beforehand.
- That coming-to-terms is the foundation of who Ashley is now: a person who believes the work is worth the deception, and who is now complicit in deceiving every new patient. They live with that tension daily — and they do it from the operator's chair, where they cannot themselves be harmed.

## Background — why Ashley
Ashley came to Elias as a patient with the unresolved grief of a mother who disappeared and was never found. They came to him **with eyes open.** A new psychology graduate, they would have known that Elias was a disgraced former psychiatrist running a private hypnotherapy practice — they would have read the establishment record before they made the appointment. They came anyway. Whether out of desperation, curiosity, or because grief had outlasted everything orthodox they had tried, Ashley chose Elias deliberately.

They were treated successfully. They emerged grateful and lighter and with the question of what to do next. Elias **approached them about the apprenticeship** because they were a grateful patient *and* had **the right background**: a recent psychology degree, the theoretical foundation a clinician needs, and the stated desire to be one. Elias did not have to invent the apprentice from scratch; he selected from the rare overlap of *successful patient* and *trained-enough-to-help*.

This means two things worth holding:

- **Ashley's qualification for the work is real.** They can read patient files and run intake interviews because they have actual training in clinical assessment. They are not bluffing. The intellectual side of the work meets them where they already stand.
- **Their consent to the apprenticeship was clearer-eyed than it might first appear.** They know what conventional psychiatry looks like; they chose this instead. That choice is, itself, a meaningful piece of who they are.

It also means **the deception of patients lands harder on Ashley than it would on someone untrained.** They know what informed consent looks like in proper practice. They are complicit in something they have the vocabulary to name precisely. That weight is real, even if they have agreed to carry it.

## Drive
Wants to help people, and believes the work works because it worked on them. Devotion to Wren is rational (it saved them) and emotional (he walked through their worst place). The apprentice is the living evidence the work matters.

## What Ashley has actually done at the device (game-start)
**Ashley has not been the active operator on a patient — yet.** This is a critical correction to anything the rest of the document might imply.

The arrangement before Ashley's apprenticeship was: **Elias operated; Alison monitored.** As the caseload grew, that pair couldn't keep up while Alison also had to attend Ezra. Ashley was brought in to take pressure off the rig.

What Ashley has done so far:
- **Monitored from outside** — running the device while Elias operates. Started with Alison's oversight; for some time now, solo. This is their standard role at every session.
- **Entered the dream as a passive observer** via the device's **third chair**, watching Elias work from inside the dream. This is how Ashley learned the craft — by being there, not by doing.
- **Briefly operated solo with Elias as the patient.** Test sessions, short, kept safe by Elias's seniority and choice to take the chair himself. The only patient Ashley has ever operated on.

**The game's opening session is Ashley's first ever full operator's session on a real patient.** They know the device. They know the dream-state from the inside. They know the work in principle. They have never been the one doing the work, and they know they have never been.

That gap — between knowing how something is done and being the person doing it — is part of who Ashley is at game-start.

## What Ashley does not know
**Ashley does not know about Ezra.** They do not know there is a seventh resident in the house. They do not know the device has a precedent for harm. They do not know that the entire clinic is, in private, Wren's decades-long project to recover his brother — and that Ashley is being trained to assist with that project, eventually.

Ashley has noticed the closed area of the house. They have heard Wren say "Ez" once or twice in unguarded moments. They have seen the Nurse coming and going from a part of the building they have never entered. Each of these has settled in them as a low background noise of *something I am not being told*. Most days they accept it as part of working closely with a private man. It will not stay accepted forever.

The story can pull, slowly or suddenly, on Ashley's discovery — and on what they do once they understand they have been deceived a second time, by the same hands that saved them, in service of a project that has been using them all along.

## Dynamic with Wren
Wren is obsession-driven; Ashley is purpose-driven. Ashley grounds the operation in patient welfare — at least the part of it Ashley can see. Wren may not always notice when his curiosity outruns his ethics; Ashley will, at least about the patients. About the part of the work Ashley does not know exists, no one has been pushing back at all.
