# Season Arc / Game Structure

## Where the story begins

The narrative opens with **Ashley already established in the house but still feeling like a newcomer.** They have lived there for **roughly a year**, since the end of their own treatment.

When Ashley was healed, Elias offered them the apprenticeship. The reason he needed an apprentice is practical: until then, **Elias and Alison had operated the device together** — Elias going in, Alison monitoring from outside — but as the caseload grew, that arrangement could not scale. Alison cannot be in the monitor's chair every session and still attend Ezra. Ashley was brought in to relieve the pressure.

The reason he approached *Ashley* specifically is that they were both a successful and grateful patient *and* had the right background: a recent **bachelor's in psychology** and the stated desire to clinic. Elias was selecting from the rare overlap. (See [apprentice.md](apprentice.md).)

## What Ashley has actually done before game-start
- **Trained as the monitor** — running the device from outside while Elias operated. Initially Alison oversaw them; now they do it solo as standard.
- **Trained as a passive observer inside dreams.** The device supports a **third chair**: a person can be wired in and enter the dream-state as an observer — present, perceiving, not interacting. Ashley has gone into many of Elias's sessions this way, watching him work from inside the dream. That is how they learned the craft.
- **Brief, careful solo operating with Elias as the patient.** Test sessions, kept short, designed to confirm Ashley can hold the role of operator. Elias is the only patient Ashley has ever operated on, and only briefly.

**Ashley has never been the active operator on a patient.** They know the device, they know the dream-state, they know the work — but they have never done the actual job.

## Game-start = tutorial = Ashley's first real operator session
The opening of the game is **Ashley's first ever full operator session on a real patient.** Elias has decided they are ready. The tutorial level teaches the player the operator's controls and the operator's responsibility at the same time.

## The standard loop
Each patient case follows a rhythm:

1. **Read the patient's file.** Background, presenting issue, history. The file gives the player diagnostic clues.
2. **Interview the patient.** The patient is in residence; Ashley meets them in consulting rooms as part of the "hypnotherapy" framing. The interview surfaces emotional texture, fears, things the file doesn't say. These conversations seed the puzzle the dream will pose.
3. **Enter the dream.** Ashley operates; whoever is monitoring takes the outside chair. The dream-world is shaped by the patient's mind. The player works out, in the dream, what the intervention needs to be.
4. **Resolve the dream.** When the work is done correctly, the dream reaches a natural close and Ashley exits.
5. **The patient wakes changed.**

**Some patients require multiple sessions** because their issues are more acute. The dream-world remains broadly the same across sessions, but each one offers new objectives or different angles on the same knot. The same environment is experienced multiple times, until the patient is finally healed.

## Between sessions — backstory drip
**Scripted events between sessions slowly unravel the larger story.** Conversations Ashley overhears, things Elias lets slip, glimpses across thresholds, observations in the consulting rooms, Ryan returning from school, an unguarded "Ez" at the door of a closed room. Each scripted moment moves the player a single step closer to understanding what the work is actually about, without ever announcing itself.

## Mid-game pivot — operating on Elias
At a critical structural moment, **Ashley operates on Elias himself.**

This is the inflection point of the whole arc. Inside Elias's dream, Ashley encounters something — a presence, a place, a name, a moment from the brothers' shared past — that **begins the process of exposing Ezra's existence.** The session does not give Ashley the full picture. It gives them the first thread that cannot be ignored.

After this session, the silence around the closed area of the house starts to crack. Ashley begins to know there is something they have not been told.

## Late game — the Ezra arc
Eventually Elias decides Ashley is ready to operate on Ezra.

The role assignments:
- **Ashley** — the operator. They are the only person in the house who *can* do it.
- **Alison** — willing to monitor, but **refuses to operate on the device.** It creeps her out. She has spent her career caring for the man it broke; she will not put herself in the chair that can be put in his.
- **Elias** — refuses to operate on Ezra. Because of what happened. He has not been the active hands inside Ezra's dream since the day he came back alone, and he will not be again.

So the recovery falls to Ashley. The apprentice will operate on the man who has been the unspoken centre of the work all along.

**The first Ezra session: reassembling the shards.** Ashley enters Ezra's dream-world — likely vast, fragmentary, layered with everything Ezra is — and performs the first major intervention: gathering the shards, restoring some partition between present, memory, and imagination. Ezra wakes a little more coherent. Not whole. Not yet. But more present than he has been in decades.

## Game progression — patients and Ezra interleaved
After the first Ezra session, the game returns to its standard loop, with new patients to treat. **Each major chapter ends on an Ezra session.** Working on Ezra is the boundary point of the narrative — the climactic beat that closes one section and opens the next.

Each successive Ezra session brings him a little further back, and reveals a little more of the brothers' shared past, the development of the device, what happened that day, and the cost of everything Elias has built since.

## Endgame
By the final chapter, **the full backstory has been exposed to the player** — to Ashley, and through Ashley, to whoever is playing. **Ezra is fully healed.** The household, the operation, and every relationship inside it now stand reorganised around a man who can speak again. What that means for the practice, for the deception of patients, for Wren himself, and for Ashley is the resolution the closing material has to wrestle with.

## Open questions
- **The first patient — who they are, what they're suffering from, what their dream looks like.** This is the tutorial; it is also the player's first impression of the work.
- **What Elias is suffering from when Ashley operates on him.** Why does he need treatment? Or is this not strictly a "treatment" but something else (a planned excavation, a desperate attempt, something he has not been able to fix himself)? And what specifically does Ashley find inside?
- **The cohort of six and beyond** — the full set of patient cases the game cycles through. Each one is a self-contained dream-puzzle and a step in Ashley's growing understanding.
- **The number of Ezra sessions** — three, five, more? Each one is a chapter boundary, so the count is roughly the count of major acts.
- **The final state of the household.** When Ezra wakes, what happens to Wren, to Edith, to Alison, to the patients still in residence, to the practice itself? Is there a return to something like normalcy, or does the truth of the operation make that impossible?
