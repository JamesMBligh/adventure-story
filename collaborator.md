# The Collaborator

## Status (load-bearing facts)
- **Co-built the device with Wren** during decades of joint work.
- During development, Wren and the collaborator **took turns being the patient and the operator**, testing the device on each other.
- During one of those sessions, **the collaborator was the patient.** Wren was the operator. Wren went into their dream. Something happened. The collaborator did not come back to themselves.
- They are **alive but in a severely impacted mental state.** Whatever Wren did inside that dream, or could not do, or could not reach, broke them. They have not recovered.

## Why this is the spine of the story
- It establishes the asymmetry that defines the ethical core of the work: **the operator is structurally safe; the patient is not.**
- Wren is not just the architect of the device — he is the operator whose hands left someone he loved devastated. Every session he runs since has been with the same hands.
- Ashley does the same work, with the same device, knowing this is what Wren has already done.
- It also reframes Wren's silence: the silence is **guilt as much as grief**, and the guilt is specific. He was inside their dream when it happened.

## What it teaches the story (the rule it sets)
- **Operators cannot be harmed by the device.** Ashley is, in every session, physically and mentally safe. They will always come out.
- **Patients can be harmed.** What the operator does inside the dream — including doing nothing, missing something, being too slow, going too far — translates to real damage to the patient's waking mind.
- This makes the ethical position acute: Ashley operates from a position of guaranteed protection over a person in total vulnerability. The patient is sedated, deceived, dreaming. Ashley is awake, choosing.

## Open questions
- **Name** of the collaborator, and which name was used between them and Wren.
- **Their relationship to Wren** — partner, oldest friend, student-turned-equal, sibling, something else.
- **Where they are now** — cared for in the house, in an institution Wren funds, with family who don't know the truth.
- **The shape of the damage** — coma-like silence, locked-in awareness, personality dissolved, stuck in a recurring dream-state, fragmented but conscious.
- **What Wren went in to do** that session — were they treating something specific in the collaborator, or experimenting, or both?
- **What does Ashley know?** Anything? Just unguarded references? Or have they pieced more together?
- **Mrs. Halloran** — was at the house long enough to remember. What was she told?
