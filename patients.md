# Patient Roster — working list of maladies

A pool of psychological cases for the game's patient cohort. Each is **environment-, trauma-, or tragedy-based** — not genetic and not driven by primary chemical imbalance. All are plausibly addressable through counselling or psychotherapy in the real world, which means the device's dream-work can plausibly resolve them.

Names are provisional working IDs. Dream-world themes and goals will be designed per-case in a later pass.

---

## 1. Catherine Whitfield — concert pianist, stage fright
- Mid-40s. Successful concert pianist who froze on stage at a high-profile recital ~15 months ago and has not performed since.
- Body intact, technique intact. The freeze is psychological.
- Underlying: sustained verbal cruelty from a childhood music teacher, never integrated. The stage was the place she was most ashamed; the freeze is the buried shame surfacing.
- **Acuteness: moderate.** Suitable for game-start / tutorial level (already used in `scenes/the_offer.md`).

## 2. Joseph Lamb — survivor's guilt
- Late 50s. Sole survivor of a bus crash a decade ago in which seven others died.
- Develops ritualistic counting of doorways before passing through them. Cannot enter most rooms without performing the ritual.
- Underlying: a private bargain — the counting is "keeping the dead with him." If he stops, he loses them again.
- **Acuteness: moderate-to-acute.** Compulsion is interfering with daily life.

## 3. Helena Marsh — post-stillbirth avoidance
- Early 30s. Lost a daughter to stillbirth two years ago. Has not entered the prepared nursery since. Avoids prams, infants, maternity floors.
- Underlying: the loss has not been integrated; the prepared room contains a future that cannot be allowed to exist.
- **Acuteness: moderate.** A grief case where the work is letting the room be entered, not letting the loss be forgotten.

## 4. Thomas Aldridge — inherited phobia
- 40s. Severe and disabling fear of crows that has crept up on him over years.
- In intake, recalls his late mother's identical fear, drilled into him in childhood through her own avoidance behaviours.
- Underlying: the fear is not his; it is inherited. The work is recognising it as not-self.
- **Acuteness: mild-to-moderate.** A clean case for an apprentice still learning the texture of the work.

## 5. Margot Reeves — stalker aftermath
- Late 30s. Cannot be in a room alone with the door closed. The man who stalked her for years died of natural causes eighteen months ago. Her body has not been told.
- Underlying: vigilance that no longer has a referent, baked into the nervous system as habit.
- **Acuteness: moderate-to-acute.** An anxiety case where the threat is gone but the architecture of fear has not been dismantled.

## 6. Iris Cobb — functional mutism
- 50s. A schoolteacher who one afternoon, six years ago, stopped speaking aloud. Has not spoken since. Communicates by writing.
- An incident occurred that day. She has refused to describe it to any clinician.
- Underlying: conversion / dissociative response to trauma she has chosen to seal.
- **Acuteness: severe.** Late-game case. The dream may be the only place the incident can be approached.

## 7. Dr. Naomi Halpern — health anxiety
- Late 40s. A cardiologist who has come to believe she is dying. Every test clear. The certainty has not moved.
- Underlying: a fear with a different origin than the body — possibly a parent's death she watched, possibly the long privilege of certainty in her work, ending.
- **Acuteness: moderate.** A high-functioning patient whose work makes her especially difficult to reassure.

## 8. Walter Ennis — chronic self-sufficiency
- Mid-60s. Has built his life on never needing anyone. Any kindness offered to him produces physical revulsion — nausea, withdrawal, sometimes anger.
- Underlying: severe early-childhood neglect; the self-sufficiency was survival, and it has hardened into a wall against being a person who can be cared for.
- **Acuteness: moderate.** A bittersweet case — what he needs is nothing dramatic, only to be permitted to receive a single thing without flinching.

## 9. Rebecca Solovey — fear of becoming the abuser
- Early 30s, pregnant. Survivor of childhood abuse by her father. Convinced she will recreate the abuse on her own child. She would not. The conviction is not yielding to evidence.
- Underlying: the unconscious mind has not separated her from her father; the figure of the abuser and the figure of the parent have collapsed into one.
- **Acuteness: severe.** Late-game case with real urgency — the child is coming.

## 10. Daniel Aksoy — moral injury
- Late 30s. Former aid worker. Was complicit by omission in an incident at a refugee camp three years ago. Cannot look at himself in any mirror.
- Underlying: he is not the worst person in the story, and that is the part he cannot reconcile. He is asking to be punished by people who have moved on.
- **Acuteness: moderate-to-severe.** A case about witness, not absolution.

## 11. Nadia Pell — cult survivor
- Mid-40s. Left a fringe religious community after fifteen years. Cannot make a single decision without seeking inner permission from the dead leader.
- Underlying: the architecture of obedience installed across a lifetime; her own preferences feel forbidden.
- **Acuteness: severe.** Long-term case; multiple sessions likely needed.

## 12. Edmund Faye — the good son
- Late 30s. Has organised his entire life around the approval of his late father — career, marriage, hobbies. Recently realised he does not know who he himself is. Mild but persistent depression, free-floating dread.
- Underlying: a self never permitted to form. The grief is not for his father; it is for the version of himself he never met.
- **Acuteness: mild-to-moderate.** A philosophical case suitable for mid-game.

## 13. Owen Tyrrell — the brother in the river
- 50s. As a boy, watched his younger brother drown in a river. Now a father; will not let his own children near water.
- Underlying: the boy in him is still standing on the bank, and is now standing for both himself and his children.
- **Acuteness: moderate.** Clean structural case — the child-self in the dream needs to be reached, not changed.

## 14. Olwen Brett — the closeted widow
- Early 70s. Husband died eight months ago after a long marriage. Since his death she cannot be still: walks the streets at all hours, cannot stay in a room.
- Underlying: she lived her entire adult life in a marriage she chose to escape something she could never name even to herself. The thing she escaped is no longer being escaped from. She does not know what to do with herself.
- **Acuteness: moderate-to-severe.** A late-life case with real tenderness — the work is permitting her to know what she has always known, after fifty years of not knowing.

## 15. Adam Crowe — the surgeon's failure
- Late 40s. Lost a routine patient on the table eighteen months ago. The death was reviewed and not his fault. He has not operated since.
- Underlying: perfectionism as identity; the failure is unrecoverable not because it was wrong but because it was possible.
- **Acuteness: moderate.** A clean case about accepting fallibility.

## 16. Margaret Knell — the empty house
- Late 50s. Empty-nester. Three children grown and gone. Has slipped into a quiet, unconfronted depression of identity. Cannot identify what she is for now.
- Underlying: a self defined entirely through caregiving, with no second life prepared for after.
- **Acuteness: mild-to-moderate.** A common-enough case to ground the practice in real, ordinary suffering.

## Notes for designing dream-worlds (next pass)
For each case, the dream-world should:
- Be **shaped by the specific patient's psyche** — imagery, geography, and logic emerge from their inner life, not a generic template.
- Contain a **knot** — a place, figure, object, or moment that is the resolution point.
- Demand a **specific kind of intervention** — find-and-change, conversation, witness, hold-and-stay, or other.
- Be **suitable for the patient's acuteness** — milder cases get cleaner, more contained dreams; severe cases sprawl and resist.
- Pair the **strangeness flavour** to the patient — dreamlike-mythic, cosmic-unknowable, biological-organic, surreal-mundane (see `dreamworlds.md`).
