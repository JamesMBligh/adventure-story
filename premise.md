# Premise

## Core hook
A disgraced psychiatrist, **Dr. Elias Wren**, has co-built a device that lets a trained operator enter a patient's dream while the patient sleeps in REM. The dream is shaped by the patient's own mind, and **the work performed inside the dream is itself the therapy** — what the operator does in there directly changes the patient's waking psyche. Patients believe they are receiving hypnotherapy. They are not. The operator is **Ashley**, a former patient and Wren's apprentice.

## Through-line
**Helping their patients.** Each case is, in effect, an expedition into another mind.

## Tone
**Weird / surreal.** The flavor of strangeness shifts to match the patient's psyche — dreamlike-mythic, cosmic-unknowable, biological-organic, surreal-mundane all on the table depending on the case.

## The risk lives only with the patient
Because the dream IS the therapy, the device writes onto the patient's mind every time. Wanted changes (releasing grief, dissolving compulsion) and unwanted changes (damage, displacement, devastation) are the same mechanism operating at different doses.

**The operator is safe. The patient is not.** Ashley always comes back from a dream intact. The patient might not come back as themselves.

This is the ethical spine of the story. There is one historical data point on the worst case: **the collaborator**. During development, Wren and the collaborator alternated as operator and patient. The collaborator was the patient when the harm happened; Wren was the operator. The collaborator is alive but mentally devastated. Wren has continued the work since, with the same hands. See `collaborator.md`.

Ashley does this work knowing all of that. They are not in danger. The person sedated and dreaming on the other chair is.

## Portal rules (established)
- The **device** is what creates and sustains the portals.
- Only a **small number** of worlds can be sustained simultaneously — there's a hard cap.
- Implication: **triage pressure.** Choosing which patient to help, when, and at what cost.

## Structural implications for the game
- **Episodic case structure** — each patient is a world / mission / chapter.
- **Hub layer** — the clinic / lab, where the protagonist manages caseload, talks to patients pre/post-session.
- **Persistent constraints** — limited concurrent portals, patient time, device limits, MC's own toll.
- **Anthology of strangeness** — every world looks/feels different because every mind is different.

## Open questions
- Who is the protagonist personally? (Name, age, history, why this work, what haunts them)
- Is the technology legal / public / secret / contraband?
- Does MC enter dream-worlds alone, or is the patient also present in some form?
- Is the patient aware the MC is inside their dream? Do they meet, hide, fight?
- What are the **risks** to MC inside a world? What happens if they fail / get lost / die there?
- Is there an overarching antagonist or mystery, or is it pure case-of-the-week?
- Where is the device's origin — did MC invent it alone, inherit it, steal it?
- Is there a moral/ethical opposition (regulators, ethics board, rival, cult)?
