# The Device

## Form
A **chair-and-headset rig with three seats** plus an outside monitoring station:

- **Patient's chair.** The patient is wired in, sedated, and induced into **REM sleep**. Their dreaming mind hosts the world.
- **Operator's chair.** The active user — wired in, conscious, present in the dream and able to interact with it. The operator is the one doing the work.
- **Observer's chair.** A third position. The observer is wired in and enters the dream-state, but **passively** — present, perceiving, not interacting. This is how an apprentice learns the craft: by being inside a session without being responsible for it. Ashley has spent many hours in this chair watching Elias work.
- **The monitor.** A person at the rig's instruments outside, not wired in. Reads vitals, manages sedation, watches for trouble, can pull the operator (and observer, if present) out via the standard exit mechanism. This is the third-person-watching-from-outside that the device requires for safety.

A complete session is therefore patient + operator + monitor at minimum, with an optional observer. All three wired-in consciousnesses meet inside the dream the patient generates.

## Operating arrangement at game-start
- **Standard sessions for years:** Elias operated, Alison monitored.
- **Last year, since Ashley's apprenticeship:** Elias operated, **Ashley monitored** (initially with Alison's oversight, now solo). Alison freed for full attendance on Ezra. Ashley sometimes added the observer chair to learn from inside.
- **Game-start:** Ashley is moving into the operator's chair for the first time on a real patient. Whoever monitors them, monitors a session in which the most experienced person at the device is no longer the one going in.

## Operation requires external monitoring
The device + the session **need a third person watching from outside** to manage the patient's vitals, sedation, and the rig's instruments. The monitor protects the patient, not the operator. Without monitoring, the patient can be left in a state nobody is reading, and that is where bad things have already happened.

## Who normally operates
- **Ashley is the regular user.** They go into the dreams.
- **Wren operates and monitors from outside.** He is the architect of the device but no longer the one inside; presumably the toll is too much for him at his age, and his role is to read instruments, manage the patient, and pull Ashley out if needed.

## The original sin
When Wren first treated Ashley, he had **no monitor.** He sedated Ashley, wired them in, wired himself in, and went into their dream alone with the device unattended on the outside. He broke the safety rule. This is the foundational fact of his bond with Ashley:
- Ashley was the one in jeopardy that night. Wren chose to risk **them** — not himself — without a monitor. The risk was theirs, not his, and he took it on their behalf without their knowledge.
- He had already done worse, with the same hands, to the collaborator.
- Ashley learned the truth about all of it after the fact. That they had been deceived, that the device had a precedent for harm, that Wren had ignored the safety rule for them. They stayed anyway. That is the foundation.

## What the device actually does (load-bearing)
**The dream journey IS the therapy.** What the operator does inside the patient's dream directly affects the patient's waking psyche. The device is not a diagnostic tool whose insights inform later talk-therapy. The work happens in the dream. When the operator resolves a thing inside, the patient is changed when they wake.

Every use of the device **writes onto the patient's mind.** Wanted changes (releasing grief, dissolving compulsion) and unwanted changes (residue, displacement, damage) are the same mechanism operating at different doses. The device cannot heal without changing, and it cannot change without risk.

## The asymmetry — load-bearing rule
**The operator is safe. The patient is not.**

- The operator (Ashley) cannot be harmed by the device. Whatever happens in the dream-world, Ashley always comes back, intact in body and mind.
- The patient is the only one in jeopardy. The session can heal them, fail to help them, or damage them — and damage means real, lasting harm to the patient's waking mind.
- This is the ethical spine of the story. Ashley operates from guaranteed protection over a person in total vulnerability — sedated, deceived, dreaming.

## Why this rule exists
Wren and the collaborator alternated as patient and operator during development. The collaborator was harmed when they were **the patient**. Wren has not been harmed by the device, then or since. Operator safety has been demonstrated; patient risk has been written into history.

## Toll on the operator
The only real toll is **physical exhaustion** — migraines, sleep disturbance, slow recovery between sessions. Draining work, but bounded. Ashley accumulates fatigue, not damage. The dream cannot keep them.

## Origin
- Built by **Elias Wren and his fraternal twin brother, Ezra,** as **academic research** over decades of joint work. They published, presented, fought to be taken seriously, and were not. The field met their core claim — that psychological injury could be treated by direct technical intervention — with sustained scepticism. See `history.md`.
- Elias contributed the psychiatry and the theory of dream-walking; Ezra contributed the engineering — the rig, the headset, the sedation protocols, the biological interface, the apparatus itself. **Neither of them could have built it alone.**
- The early device worked but was **crude and lacked safeguards** the brothers did not yet know were needed.
- During development the brothers took turns as operator and patient, testing the device on each other. Frustrated at not being believed, they pushed for results — taking sessions beyond what they had reason to think was safe.
- During one of those sessions, with Ezra as patient and Elias as operator, something went wrong inside the dream. Ezra did not come back to himself. He is alive but mentally devastated. See `collaborator.md`.
- **Ezra's injury was the accident that ended the brothers' research and cost Elias his license** — human experimentation without ethics approval, evidenced by the harm. The current safeguards in the rig — three chairs, monitor's role, exit phrase, the safety protocols — are products of catastrophe, not foresight. They exist because Ezra paid for them.
- **In the years after the accident, Elias rebuilt and revalidated the device.** He spent years testing the rebuilt rig on animals across multiple species, culminating in a chimpanzee, before resuming human trials. The current device is therefore a *second-generation* apparatus: same basic invention, but with safety protocols developed and proven in the wake of the harm. See `history.md`.
- **Elias never speaks of him directly.** Only occasional, unguarded references slip out — *"the work we did together"*, *"that was his design"*, *"he would have known"*. Ashley pieces him together over time. Once or twice, in unguarded moments, Elias says **"Ez"** — the brothers' lifelong pet name for each other (Ezra called Elias **"El"**).
- Ezra's absence is a load-bearing silence in Elias's life. It is **guilt as much as grief**: Elias was inside his brother's dream when it happened. He is the one whose hands did it. He has continued the work since, with the same hands — and the work is, in private, the long search for a way back to him.

## Engineering knowledge — a slow ticking clock
**Ezra** understood the engineering side of the device better than Elias ever did. Elias keeps the rig running from Ezra's notebooks, drawings, and what he absorbed by osmosis over decades. The deepest knowledge of how the apparatus works lives inside the mind the device broke. This is why Elias cannot simply build a new device: the original is, for practical purposes, irreplaceable. Every component that fails is one he may not be able to repair on his own.

## Secrecy
**Almost no one knows the device exists.** Wren, Ashley, and presumably the vanished collaborator (wherever they are). The rest of the world has no idea.

**Critically, the patients themselves do not know.** They are told they are receiving hypnotherapy. They are hypnotised, then sedated, then connected to the device without their knowledge. They wake believing the hypnosis worked. See `clinic.md` for the full ethical and practical structure of this deception — it is the central moral fault line of the story.
