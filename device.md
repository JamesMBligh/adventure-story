# The Device

## Form
A **paired chair-and-headset rig.** Two seats:

- **Operator's chair (Ashley's chair):** the user is wired in via headset and goes consciously into the patient's dream-world.
- **Patient's chair:** the patient is wired in, sedated, and induced into **REM sleep**. Their dreaming mind hosts the world the operator enters.

Both chairs are coupled to the device. The two consciousnesses meet inside the dream the patient generates.

## Operation requires external monitoring
The device + the session **need a third person watching from outside** to manage the patient's vitals, sedation, and the rig's instruments. The monitor protects the patient, not the operator. Without monitoring, the patient can be left in a state nobody is reading, and that is where bad things have already happened.

## Who normally operates
- **Ashley is the regular user.** They go into the dreams.
- **Wren operates and monitors from outside.** He is the architect of the device but no longer the one inside; presumably the toll is too much for him at his age, and his role is to read instruments, manage the patient, and pull Ashley out if needed.

## The original sin
When Wren first treated Ashley, he had **no monitor.** He sedated Ashley, wired them in, wired himself in, and went into their dream alone with the device unattended on the outside. He broke the safety rule. This is the foundational fact of his bond with Ashley:
- Ashley was the one in jeopardy that night. Wren chose to risk **them** — not himself — without a monitor. The risk was theirs, not his, and he took it on their behalf without their knowledge.
- He had already done worse, with the same hands, to the collaborator.
- Ashley learned the truth about all of it after the fact. That they had been deceived, that the device had a precedent for harm, that Wren had ignored the safety rule for them. They stayed anyway. That is the foundation.

## What the device actually does (load-bearing)
**The dream journey IS the therapy.** What the operator does inside the patient's dream directly affects the patient's waking psyche. The device is not a diagnostic tool whose insights inform later talk-therapy. The work happens in the dream. When the operator resolves a thing inside, the patient is changed when they wake.

Every use of the device **writes onto the patient's mind.** Wanted changes (releasing grief, dissolving compulsion) and unwanted changes (residue, displacement, damage) are the same mechanism operating at different doses. The device cannot heal without changing, and it cannot change without risk.

## The asymmetry — load-bearing rule
**The operator is safe. The patient is not.**

- The operator (Ashley) cannot be harmed by the device. Whatever happens in the dream-world, Ashley always comes back, intact in body and mind.
- The patient is the only one in jeopardy. The session can heal them, fail to help them, or damage them — and damage means real, lasting harm to the patient's waking mind.
- This is the ethical spine of the story. Ashley operates from guaranteed protection over a person in total vulnerability — sedated, deceived, dreaming.

## Why this rule exists
Wren and the collaborator alternated as patient and operator during development. The collaborator was harmed when they were **the patient**. Wren has not been harmed by the device, then or since. Operator safety has been demonstrated; patient risk has been written into history.

## Toll on the operator
The only real toll is **physical exhaustion** — migraines, sleep disturbance, slow recovery between sessions. Draining work, but bounded. Ashley accumulates fatigue, not damage. The dream cannot keep them.

## Origin
- Built by **Wren and a collaborator** during decades of joint work.
- During development they took turns as operator and patient.
- During one session, with the collaborator as patient and Wren as operator, something went wrong inside the dream. The collaborator did not come back to themselves. They are alive but mentally devastated. See `collaborator.md`.
- **Wren never speaks of them directly.** Only occasional, unguarded references slip out — *"the work we did together"*, *"that was their design"*, *"they would have known"*. Ashley pieces them together over time.
- The collaborator's absence is a load-bearing silence in Wren's life. It is **guilt as much as grief**: Wren was inside their dream when it happened. He is the one whose hands did it. He has continued the work since, with the same hands.

## Secrecy
**Almost no one knows the device exists.** Wren, Ashley, and presumably the vanished collaborator (wherever they are). The rest of the world has no idea.

**Critically, the patients themselves do not know.** They are told they are receiving hypnotherapy. They are hypnotised, then sedated, then connected to the device without their knowledge. They wake believing the hypnosis worked. See `clinic.md` for the full ethical and practical structure of this deception — it is the central moral fault line of the story.
