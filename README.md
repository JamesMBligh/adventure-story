# Story Background Material

Working notes for a **sci-fi / speculative interactive game**.

## One-paragraph pitch
A disgraced psychiatrist, **Dr. Elias Wren**, has built a device that lets a trained operator enter a patient's dream-world while the patient sleeps in REM. His apprentice, **Ashley** — once a patient themselves — is now the operator. Together they take cases the establishment couldn't help, working out of Wren's converted home. Each patient's mind is a different impossible world. Patients believe they are receiving hypnotherapy; they are not. The player is Ashley, going in.

## Hard rules (enforce in any future writing)
- **Ashley's sex/gender is never revealed.** Pronouns: they/them. Description must stay ambiguous.
- **The dream IS the therapy.** What Ashley does inside the dream directly changes the patient's waking mind. Every session writes onto the patient.
- **The risk is asymmetric and one-sided. The operator (Ashley) is structurally safe. The patient is not.** Ashley always comes back from a dream intact, in body and mind. Patients can be healed, unhelped, or harmed. The collaborator is the precedent — they were the *patient* when the worst case occurred. The operator's only toll is physical exhaustion. This asymmetry is the ethical spine of the story; do not undermine it.
- The device's existence is a secret kept from patients, the public, and Mrs. Halloran. Documentation = prison. Secrecy is a precondition for the work, not a preference.

## Established (load-bearing facts)
- Format: Game / interactive
- Genre: Sci-fi / speculative, weird-surreal tone
- Setting: Present day, large house with grounds owned by Wren, near an unspecified city
- Player character: Ashley (always seen as themselves inside dreams)
- Hub character: Wren (operates / monitors from outside, two-way voice comms with Ashley)
- Caseload: up to 6 in-patients in residence at a time, weekly cycle, multiple sessions per patient
- The device was built by Wren and a collaborator, who was left alive but mentally devastated by an accident during development; Wren never speaks of them
- Wren broke the safety rule (no monitor) for Ashley's own treatment — the foundation of their bond
- Wren has continued the work knowing the worst case has already happened once

## Major open threads
- **The collaborator** — name, what they were to Wren, where they are now, what the accident specifically was.
- **Wren's heretical theory** — what specifically did he claim that got him cast out? It implies the *object of his obsession* — what he is really trying to prove.
- **The current cohort of six patients** — none yet sketched. The first/tutorial case especially.
- **Long arc** — antagonist, escalation, the toll line that gets crossed, the question the season answers.
- **Wren's personal life pre-clinic** — family, marriages, children. He didn't begin alone.
- **The clinic's money** — Wren is disgraced and off-grid. What pays for the house, the staff, the device?
- **Ashley's current depletion** — how many cases in are they at game-start? Recent close calls?
- **The exit phrase** — small but specific texture detail.
