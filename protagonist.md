# Protagonist — Dr. Elias Wren

## Core
- **Name:** Dr. Elias Wren
- **Profession:** Psychiatrist
- **Age:** Late 50s+
- **Gender:** Man
- **Status:** Disgraced / on the fringe — but more "rejected disruptor" than "ethical scandal." He challenged the establishment with ideas it wasn't ready to absorb, and it threw him out.

## Drive
**Scientific obsession.** Wren is hunting a real answer to what consciousness is. Patients are how he funds the answer, and how he morally justifies the work — but the obsession is the engine. He doesn't fully see, or won't admit, where helping ends and using begins.

## Backstory beats (working)
- Distinguished early career, then a public break with the field.
- Published work or theory the establishment branded pseudoscience or unethical-by-implication.
- Has been right about something fundamental — and the resentment runs both ways.
- Continues practicing in some marginal/grey space; the device is his real laboratory.

## Personality (working)
- Brilliant, patient, courteous — and unbendable on the work.
- Old-school clinician's manner: listens carefully, remembers everything.
- Underneath: vanity, pride, a deep need to be vindicated before he dies.

## Relationships
- **The apprentice** (see `apprentice.md`) — his only inner-circle figure. Former patient, mid-20s, sharp and earnest.
- **The collaborator** (see `collaborator.md`) — co-builder of the device. During a development session in which the collaborator was the patient and Wren was the operator, something went wrong inside the dream. The collaborator did not come back to themselves. They are alive but devastated. Wren never speaks of them. The silence is **guilt as much as grief, and the guilt is specific**: Wren was inside their dream when it happened. The hands that did it are still doing the work.
