# Protagonist — Dr. Elias Wren

## Core
- **Name:** Dr. Elias Wren
- **Profession:** Psychiatrist
- **Age:** Late 50s+
- **Gender:** Man
- **Status:** Disgraced. Medical license revoked. Research discredited. Operates a "hypnotherapy" practice out of his family home, off the official record. The disgrace is real and bureaucratic, but quiet — the story was contained at the time and never widely known. See [history.md](history.md).

## Drive
**He is trying to undo what he did to the collaborator.**

Everything Wren does — the device, the clinic, the deceit, the relentless caseload, the willingness to break every rule including ones written in his own hand — exists in service of one private project: finding a way back to the person he broke. The collaborator is alive, somewhere reachable, in a state Wren has been failing to recover for years. Each patient case is therapy for the patient and **a test bench for techniques he might one day try on the collaborator.** What he learns from helping a stranger is what he hopes to bring home.

The "scientific obsession" framing is true on the surface. Underneath it is grief and guilt with a target. He is not seeking knowledge for its own sake; he is seeking the one specific thing knowledge could buy him. This is also why he takes risks no responsible practitioner would: he has already lost the thing that mattered most. Anything else he might lose has to be measured against that.

## Archetype
The brilliant, fallen, obsessive scientist as **Orpheus** — going down again and again into other people's underworlds, learning the geography, looking for the rule that lets him bring his own person back. Patients are simultaneously human beings he wants to help and rare opportunities to advance the work that matters more than anything to him personally. He does not fully see, or will not admit, where helping ends and using begins. He may genuinely not know himself.

## Backstory beats
**The disgrace is the accident.** Elias did not lose his standing for being heretical — he lost it for what the establishment correctly identified as an ethical breach: human experimentation on his brother, without proper ethics approval, that left a man permanently injured. The medical board was technically right. They were also wrong about the deeper thing — they never believed the device worked, so they never confiscated it, never investigated what was really there. The theoretical claim and the ethical breach got branded together as "fringe pseudo-research" and were quietly buried. See [history.md](history.md) for the full timeline.

What Elias retains:
- The family home, the estate, the device.
- His expertise.
- Ezra, alive but broken, in the parents' suite.

What he does not retain:
- A medical license.
- Standing to publish or be cited.
- Institutional protection of any kind.

The clinic operates as a private hypnotherapy practice — a thin legal shield around what is actually happening. Disclosure of the device would mean prison, not just a second disgrace.

## Personality (working)
- Brilliant, patient, courteous — and unbendable on the work.
- Old-school clinician's manner: listens carefully, remembers everything.
- Underneath: vanity, pride, a deep need to be vindicated before he dies.

## Relationships
- **The apprentice** (see [apprentice.md](apprentice.md)) — Ashley. Former patient, mid-20s, sharp and earnest. The only person he is now in unguarded daily company with.
- **Ezra Wren, his fraternal twin brother** (see [collaborator.md](collaborator.md)). Co-builder of the device: Elias contributed the mind, Ezra contributed the machine. Neither could have built it alone. During a development session in which Ezra was the patient and Elias was the operator, something went wrong inside the dream. Ezra did not come back to himself. He is alive but devastated, kept somewhere in the house. The brothers called each other **Ez** and **El** all their lives — Elias is now the only person alive who uses either name, and almost never aloud. The silence is **guilt as much as grief, and the guilt is specific**: Elias was inside Ezra's dream when it happened. The hands that did it are still doing the work — and the work is the long, private project of finding a way back to him.
