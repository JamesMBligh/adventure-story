# Dream-Worlds — Rules and Mechanics

## What they are
A dream-world is the patient's REM-sleep dream, made navigable to a second consciousness by the device. Each world is **shaped by the specific patient** — their psychology, their unresolved knot, their imagery. Strangeness flavor varies per case (dreamlike-mythic, cosmic, biological, surreal-mundane — anything the mind generates).

## Critical: the dream IS the therapy
What Ashley does inside the dream-world **directly changes the patient's waking mind.** The dream is not a diagnostic surface that informs later talk-therapy; the work happens *in* the dream. Resolve the knot inside and the patient wakes resolved. Damage something inside and the patient wakes damaged.

This is the thing that makes every choice load-bearing. Ashley is not an observer; Ashley is operating on a living mind in real time. Wrong action inside causes real-world harm. There is no rehearsal, only practice with consequences.

## The operator's form inside
**The operator appears as themselves.** Their own body, their own face, their own clothes. They walk into the dream as a real-looking stranger.

This is significant:
- The patient (their dream-self) **can perceive the operator**, and registers them as someone-not-of-this-dream.
- Different patients react differently — some accept the operator as part of the world; some try to make sense of them inside the dream-logic; some treat them as intruder.
- The operator has to navigate "I am the only thing here that knows it is a dream" without breaking the dream prematurely.

## The observer's form inside
The device's **third chair** allows a passive observer to enter the dream alongside the operator. The observer is present and perceiving — they see what the operator sees, hear what the operator hears, can move freely — but they **cannot interact** with the dream. The patient's dream-self does not see them. They cannot pick anything up, change anything, or be touched. They are a witness, not an actor.

This is how Ashley trained. They went into many of Elias's sessions in the observer's chair, watching the work from inside without bearing its weight. It is also the configuration in which two trusted people can debrief a session by reference to the same experience — one of them can say *"do you remember the room with the broken clock?"* and mean exactly the same room.

The observer can speak with the operator via the standard two-way comms (subvocalised; routed through the rig). They cannot speak to the patient or affect anything in the dream-world. To the dream itself, the observer simply does not exist.

## What Ashley does inside (the work)
**Different per case.** No two minds need the same operation. Part of the diagnostic skill — and the gameplay — is figuring out what kind of intervention this dream needs:
- **Find and change** — an object, a place, or a moment, that if altered releases the real-world block.
- **Hold a conversation** — with the patient or with a figure standing in for someone they couldn't speak to in life.
- **Witness and validate** — see what the patient cannot bear to see, so the patient can integrate it.
- **Other shapes** — a dream may demand survival, choice, refusal, or the simple act of staying.

Reading the dream — what it is asking for — is the heart of the work.

## The asymmetry of risk
**Ashley cannot be harmed.** Whatever the dream does, whatever the patient's dream-self does, however lost a place feels — Ashley always comes back, intact in body and mind. The device was demonstrated safe for the operator during development.

**The patient is the one in jeopardy.** What Ashley does inside the dream — including doing nothing, missing something, being too slow, getting it wrong — can leave the patient damaged when they wake. The collaborator is the precedent: a session in which the operator's actions left the patient in a state they did not return from. See [collaborator.md](collaborator.md).

This is the ethical spine of the story. Ashley walks into a sleeping mind from a position of guaranteed safety. The patient is sedated, deceived, and dreaming. Every choice Ashley makes inside is a choice made *from* protection *over* someone in total vulnerability. There is no symmetric risk to balance the responsibility.

## Time
**Variable per dream.** Each patient's mind runs its own clock. Some sessions are 1:1; some run hours-inside-for-minutes-outside; some go the other way. Ashley cannot predict the ratio when entering. Wren reads the instruments and tracks elapsed outside-time — Ashley relies on Wren to know how long they've been in.

## Exit
Two ways out:
1. **Ashley speaks the prearranged phrase.** A specific sentence the device listens for — speaking it inside the dream triggers extraction.
2. **Wren pulls them out from the rig.** From outside, Wren can initiate an emergency exit at any time.

Failure mode: if Ashley **cannot speak** (gagged, drowning, voiceless, in a place with no air) and Wren **cannot tell** they need out, Ashley stays. The two-redundant-paths model has a gap, and the gap is where the danger lives.

## Comms with Wren
**Two-way voice.** Wren can speak to Ashley as an interior voice; Ashley can subvocalize back. Wren is, effectively, the player's constant companion — the calm voice in the ear, reading instruments, naming what time has done outside, asking the right question. He is also the constant moral checkpoint.

Comms can fail: dream interference, the patient's mind dampening it, Ashley going somewhere even Wren can't reach. Loss of comms is itself a warning sign.

## Patient memory of Ashley
**Patients remember Ashley.** They wake recalling a vivid dream in which a stranger appeared and walked through their world. Wren manages this during the "hypnosis" debrief — a graceful redirection along the lines of *"that figure was your subconscious externalizing the part of you that already knew the answer."* Most patients accept this.

This is a **standing risk thread.** Two patients comparing dream-stories at the dinner table, or one patient describing Ashley too accurately, or a patient remembering more than Wren can fold away — any of those is a fracture point.

## Re-entry
**Same dream, persistent.** The patient's mind hosts the same dream-world across multiple sessions over their week. Landmarks remain, figures remain, geography remains. Ashley can plan, return, finish what they started over a several-session arc. Each patient case is therefore not one dive but a small expedition.

## Open questions
- The exact wording of the exit phrase. (Worth a ritual of its own.)
- What Ashley carries in beyond their own body and clothes. Is it possible to bring an object? Notes? Has anyone tried?
- What Ashley brings out: do they remember the dream as clearly as a waking event, or does it fade? (Affects how Wren documents cases — testimony vs. recording.)
- Whether the patient's mind ever **refuses** the device — has someone simply not produced a navigable dream?
