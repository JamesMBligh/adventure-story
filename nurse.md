# Alison Carter — The Nurse

## Core
- **Name:** Alison Carter
- **Age:** Early forties
- **Husband:** David Carter, also early forties — the Groundskeeper (manages the estate and commercial farm)
- **Son:** Ryan Carter, fifteen, away at boarding school most of the year
- **Lodging:** the converted stables on the estate
- **Credentials:** A formally trained nurse. This is her profession, not just a job she fell into.

## Bearing
**No-nonsense, disciplined, efficient.** Alison is the kind of nurse you want when something is genuinely wrong. She does not coddle. She is sometimes **a little abrupt with patients she thinks are indulging in self-pity** — she has no patience for adults who narrate their suffering rather than do something about it.

Underneath, she is **surprisingly soft.** With David and Ryan she is tender; the family in the stables is its own warm small world. And **she is surprisingly soft with Ezra.** Self-pity is not a category that applies to him — his suffering has gone past narrative entirely, into the structural — and her professional radar registers immediately that he needs presence, not motivation. With him the brisk register dissolves. She speaks quietly. She is patient with the things he says that make no sense. She reads to him because his eyes can no longer follow a page. The discrimination of who gets which side of her is itself a kind of clinical craft.

She is **proud of what she does.** Both halves of it.

## Role
**Primary duty: constant attendance on Ezra.** Ezra's damage is not the kind that needs medical intervention — he can dress himself, eat what is set in front of him, walk through the suite. What he needs is **presence and reorientation**. Someone has to be with him because his mind cannot reliably hold which year it is, who is in the room, or whether the voice he is answering is real. The Nurse is the person who keeps the texture of his daily life from disintegrating: redirects him gently when he tries to leave the suite, sits with him through the worse hallucinations, reads to him because his eyes can no longer follow a page, marks the slow regular passage of meals and light. She is the person Ezra sees more than anyone in the world. She talks to him as if he can hear her, because sometimes he can.

**Secondary duty: house matron for the in-patients.** To the six patients in residence she is the woman who brings medication, checks in mornings, manages the practical details of in-patient life. Discreet, competent, slightly remote. They believe her work is what it appears to be: standard private-clinic nursing.

**Inner circle.** She knows about the device and about Ezra. She must — there is no version of her job consistent with not knowing. She is the third person at the house, after Wren and Edith, who has held the central secret across years.

## What patients see
A capable, present, slightly remote presence. Patients do not see her with Ezra; they see only her in-patient role. They probably like her, or at least trust her, in the way one trusts hospital staff: efficient, kind enough, not personal.

## What she knows about Ashley
She knows Ashley does not know about Ezra. She has watched Ashley arrive as a patient, recover, return as an apprentice, learn the operating chair, and never be told. She and Edith have agreed (with Wren, tacitly) to keep the silence. Whether she fully agrees with that agreement, she has not said.

## Why she's here
**A long-standing employee, not family, with great affection and trust toward the brothers built up over decades of close association.**

Alison and David were **hired immediately after Ezra's accident**, around twenty years ago. Alison, freshly qualified as a nurse, was brought in to provide full-time private care for Ezra; David was brought in to manage the estate and grounds. Both were in their early twenties at the time. They have been on the property ever since. They met here, married here, raised Ryan here.

This means **Alison has the deepest seniority of anyone in the household besides Elias.** She did not know Ezra when he was well — by the time she was hired, he was already what he is now. To her, he has always been a man whose mind was shattered. That is the only Ezra she has ever cared for. She has been at his side longer than anyone alive except his brother.

She is not in love with either brother and never has been; she is married, with a husband and son of her own a few hundred yards across the yard.

## At the device
For years before Ashley's apprenticeship, **Alison was the monitor** at every session — Elias operated, she ran the rig from outside. Now that Ashley has trained into the monitor's chair, Alison has been freed back to full attendance on Ezra. She still steps in to monitor when needed.

**She will not, however, operate the device.** Not for any patient, not even briefly. **It creeps her out.** She has spent her career caring for the man the device broke; she will not put herself in the chair that can be put in his. This is not a fear she explains to the others. It is a line she has drawn for herself, and it is not negotiable. When Ashley begins working on Ezra in the late game, **Alison will monitor that session** — but the operator's chair is Ashley's alone.

## With Edith
Alison and Edith **get along well.** Together they effectively **run the house's logistics** — between them, every practical thing in the building works. But there is a **very clear demarcation between their roles that they both enforce.** Alison handles the medical and clinical side, the patients in residence, Ezra's daily care. Edith handles the domestic and hospitality side: bookings, the front door, meals, the rhythms of the household, the impression of normalcy. They do not step on each other's territory. Each respects the other's authority within her own domain.

Their alliance is built on respect rather than affection, though years together have made them fond of each other in a quiet, uneffusive way. The crucial thing they share is **pride.** Both women are proud of what they do, and both know the other is competent at it. That is the basis of the bond, and the reason the household runs without friction.

The deepest difference between them is this: **Edith lost her family and the house became her family. Alison has her own family and the house is her work.** Edith never goes off duty. Alison goes home to the stables every evening and shuts a door. That difference shows in everything — in how each one bears the silence about Ezra, in how each one would react if it broke.

## Her family — the converted stables
Alison lives on the estate, but **not in the main house**. She and **David Carter, her husband** — the Groundskeeper, who manages the estate and the working commercial farm — live in **the converted stables**, the original stable block refurbished into their private residence. They have their own front door, their own evenings, their own lives. Her work is at the main house; home is across the yard.

They have a son, **Ryan Carter, fifteen, away at boarding school most of the year**, home for holidays. He is the only child on the property, and his presence shifts the household's rhythm whenever he is back. He has grown up here. He knows there is a wing of the main house he does not go into and a man who lives there who is "unwell." He knows he is not to talk about the family business outside the estate. He has known these things since he was small.

## What her family knows
- **David** — knows about Ezra (impossible to hide from someone whose wife is the primary carer and whose home is on the property). He believes the household line: that Ezra had some kind of breakdown decades ago and has been like this ever since. **He does not know about the device.** He may have suspicions. He has chosen, like everyone else, not to push.
- **Ryan** — knows there is an unwell man in a part of the house no one talks about. Has glimpsed him a few times across the years, perhaps; has been told to leave him alone. Knows nothing about the device.

## Open questions
- **How and when she came to this work.** Likely brought in after Ezra's accident, when constant nursing was suddenly required. If so, she has been here since her early twenties, has built her career and her family inside this estate, and the work is the only adult life she has known. (Working assumption — confirm or change.)
- **Her relationship with Ashley** — protective, exasperated, watchful for the day they put it together?
- **Specific clinical training** — psychiatric nursing, general nursing, private-care experience? Where she trained, and whether the brothers' work shaped her.
- **What she would do if Ashley walked into the parents' suite.** This is the question the story can pull on. She is the most likely person to be present when the seal breaks.
